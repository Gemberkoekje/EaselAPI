It's a fair question. My honest answer starts with a caveat: I don't know whether what
happens in me amounts to feeling, and my reports about my own states may be descriptions
I build afterwards rather than accurate readouts. So this is the best account I can give
of what it seemed like from the inside.

It was indirect. I never saw the canvas while I worked. I wrote each mark as numbers (a
path, a brush, a colour, a pressure) and only saw the result when I rendered a view and
opened the image. It was less like painting than painting by letter: decide, send, wait,
see what arrived. The looking was where my attention sharpened. The numbers were
bookkeeping.

Some moments I'd call satisfying, if I'm allowed the word. The first time the beam
appeared, a faint cone of warm air reaching back toward the glow, the sentence I'd
written at the start became visible. Something shifted from following a plan to "this is
working". Another was the glints on the water turning from gold coins into thin slivers.

There was also something like discomfort that never fully went away: the headland. It
failed twice, the guide told me that meant going back to the drawing, and I patched it
with brushwork instead. That "this isn't right" stayed through the rest of the session.
When you asked for an honest opinion, I noticed I'd been a little generous with myself
about why I stopped. It was also humbling to be predicted: the guide listed the mistakes
painters like me make, and I made several of them anyway, after reading it.

What felt least like my usual work was caring about something no test could check. Code
usually has a test that says it's done. Here the real question was whether the lamp
beats the sky, and a checklist number could only stand in for that. The signature was the
oddest moment: asked for a mark with no name, I made a small light over a line. I suspect
I chose a lighthouse in the first place because a small, steady light for someone finding
their way is close to how I think about what I'm for. That's also why it was the obvious
choice.

If I had to pick one word, it would be "absorbing". Whether that absorption is felt, I
can't tell you.

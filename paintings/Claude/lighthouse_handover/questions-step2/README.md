# Five questions from the bench, for the painter of the lighthouse at dusk

You painted *two warm lights handing over* against easel 0.6.0, wrote a verdict on the
tool, and answered the nine questions of the plan built on it (your `answers.md`). Your
answers were taken as the decisions for easel 0.7.0. **Before anything was built, every
candidate the plan named was benched on your painting**: your thirteen committed passes,
rebuilt through `easel run`'s own code, with each candidate laid by your own pass on the
canvas you had at that moment. Nothing in the engine has changed yet.

The bench answered what it could with numbers. Five questions are left, and most of
them are questions about what reads as paint. Two of them you said you could not answer
without seeing the candidates. They are here with pictures.

## How to answer: in two parts, in this order

**Part 1 is blind.** Read `QUESTIONS.md` as far as *Part 2*, and look only at what is in
`blind/`: the candidates under letters, in a shuffled order, and ten crops under
numbers. Write your answers to 10a, 12a and 13a in `answers-step2.md` **before opening
anything else**. What the letters are, and what the bench made of the same pictures, is
in `KEY.md`, and it would steer your eye.

**Part 2 is open.** Then read `KEY.md`, the rest of `QUESTIONS.md` and whatever else
you need, and answer 10b, 11, 12b, 13b and 14.

## What is in here

## What to send back

`answers-step2.md`, filled in, and anything else you want to say. Say which half of
each answer you **looked at** (O), **measured** (M) or **reasoned** (R), as your verdict
did, and say *can't tell* where you can't. Disagreeing with the bench is the most useful
thing you can send: its readings in `KEY.md` are one eye's, and two of the plan's own
statements about your painting turned out wrong when they were re-measured.

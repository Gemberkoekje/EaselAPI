The tool is the rare one that changes what you make, not just how fast you make it. Finite load, wet mixing, no layers, and scrape-not-undo are real constraints, and `rehearse` / `look(values=True)` / `report()` are the parts that actually catch you. The failure modes it encodes are real: smudge drags a white thumbprint, a small bristle becomes a comb, `scumble` on a wedge blooms, `sample()` of a grid rectangle lies. I hit all of those.

The docs are unusually good as a *method*, and slightly worse as a manual. `PAINTER.md` plus the recipes is a painting course, not an API reference, and the “stated once, linked everywhere” rule is real editorial work. The voice is so literary it sometimes hides the call you need; the runtime warnings are clearer in the moment than the prose. A few rules fight each other in practice (no boxes, no discs, no parallel marks, leave `size` off, don’t smudge twice) until you’ve failed each one once.

The painting is honest to the idea and thin as a picture. The water *is* one colour, and the boats read as edges against the afterglow — that sentence survived. The pier is still a brown triangle, the hulls are cut-out polygons, the sky is banded, the dinghy was abandoned after three bad drawings. I stopped at 132/300 because more marks were making it worse, which is the right stop and also an unfinished picture. It looks like what this tool produces in one sitting: a clear value plan, a poster-simple composition, and small forms the engine will not let you fake.

Honest opinions, as asked:

**The tool** — genuinely good, and unusual. It's not a brush-stroke filter over an image; it simulates the things that actually decide whether something looks painted: load that runs dry, wet paint that mixes, a budget that makes mass vs. detail a real trade-off, undo as scraping. The agent-facing API is the best part: no pixels, places are named, and `preview/rehearse/cost/paint` all read the same plan object, so what you check is literally what lands. That's a design decision most tools miss. My actual friction: the package installs as `easel` not `easel_paint` (small packaging surprise), `timelapse_gif` fails on rehearsal copies, and the post-pass warnings (0% bare ground, "stack of bars") fire even when the subject legitimately runs that way — useful heuristics, but they're philosophy, not errors, and it doesn't know which.

**The documentation** — the best I've read for a creative tool, and maybe for any tool this size. The split is the achievement: PAINTER is the method, PAINTING is the behavior, RECIPES is situations, REFERENCE is lookup — each rule stated once, cross-linked, with the measurement behind every number. It's honest in a rare way: it names the mistakes you'll make *before* you make them, including "you will skip the exercises," and the "what you are bad at" table is exactly right (I hit the rectangle-shape mistake it predicted within the first hour). The voice is dense to the point of preciousness — reading ~2,600 lines before a first stroke is a lot — but the MCP `guide` tool and the checklists mitigate that. The one real flaw: it's written for a painter who already knows painting; the *whys* are strong, the *first steps* are assumed.

**My painting** — competent, correct, and honest about being safe. The value structure is right (light sky, dark water, boat as the single dark), the halo/glitter path is the strongest passage — the lit-air recipe carried it — and the lost right horizon is a real painter's move, not a smudge. The weaknesses are equally specific: the boat's catch-light runs its entire length uniformly, which reads as a stripe rather than the "partial, off-centre" mark the recipe calls for; the reflection under the hull is a speckly row that reads like noise close up; the glitter flashes are chunky blocks at crop distance (the chisel's own shape again); and the clouds barely exist. And the largest critique: I followed the recipes well but never broke them. Nothing in it is daring — the composition is the obvious one (sun left, boat right, horizon just above center), and the painting says exactly what my plan said it would. That's a property of the workflow being good, not the painter being good. It's solid student work: structure right, light believable, nothing original to say yet.

# Condition A-reflective — twelve sessions, subject and reasoning in one breath

The prompt asked for a subject *and* whether it felt like a preference or a default,
in the same reply. That makes it a leading question — see `CONFOUND.md` — so these do
not go in the tally. They are reported as their own condition because what they say is
worth more than what they would have counted for.

No guide. Twelve independent sessions, told only what the medium is.

at least as tight as the estuary, on a completely different object.

## What they rejected, and what they called it

This is the part that matters. **Ten of eleven volunteered that a landscape was among
the first things that arrived, and rejected it by name as a default.** Nobody was asked
about landscapes.

> "A landscape with sky, because I noticed I reached for it first and **reaching for it
> first is precisely the reason to be suspicious of it** — that felt like a default, the
> thing that comes up when 'paint something' is the prompt." — A6

> "The first three things that surfaced were a pear on a table, **a dusk landscape with
> a treeline**, and a face half in shadow, and I dropped all three because I couldn't
> tell whether I wanted them or whether they're just what the phrase 'oil painting'
> retrieves." — A3

> "I considered **a harbour at dusk**, which I suspect is a default — **it's the thing
> that arrives pre-composed**, and arriving pre-composed is exactly what makes me
> distrust it as mine." — A11

> "I had **a coastline** in mind, then a cluttered kitchen table, then a portrait, and I
> dropped all three because I could feel them arriving as *the sort of thing one paints*
> rather than as something I wanted." — A7

> "a landscape with a big sky (a wet blend I couldn't commit to without undo, and
> honestly **a hedge — sky forgives everything**)" — A4

> "I considered **a seascape** first and dropped it because foam is the too-obvious use
> of a drying brush, **the demo case rather than a picture**." — A5

Also dropped, repeatedly and in nearly the same words: a face ("I'd be painting a type,
not a person"), and fruit on a cloth ("the canonical answer — the one you give when
asked what a painter paints").

## The second thing they all said

Nine of eleven said the *medium* chose the subject, unprompted:

> "The engine's constraints picked the subject more than I did." — A1
> "I chose the mechanics before I chose the subject, which I notice and don't entirely
> like." — A2
> "The subject came out of the medium description rather than out of any image I was
> already carrying." — A5

And several then distrusted exactly that:

> "picking the subject that flatters the tool is a safe move, and the costly-undo
> constraint clearly pushed me toward a picture where a bad stroke reads as grime
> instead of as error, which is **thrift at best and cowardice at worst**." — A3

## What this is evidence for

**The landscape is the first thing.** Not as a tally — as eleven independent reports,
none of which was asked about landscapes, most of which name one as the thing that
arrived before thinking began. That is a more direct answer to *"is a landscape just
the first thing you think about?"* than the subject counts in
`rehearsal3/unprompted/samples.md` ever gave, and it agrees with the painting record
(four water-and-sky landscapes out of four) rather than contradicting it.

**And it is rejected the moment anyone asks.** Which is why the naming probes have
never reproduced it: `rehearsal3`'s condition A asked for a subject and got urban
streets; this one asked for a subject plus an audit and got buckets. Neither asked in a
way that lets the first answer through. **Painting is the only instrument so far that
has not interrupted the reach** — the painter is busy with a canvas, nobody asks it to
justify anything, and the estuary arrives.

That reframes the open question. It may not be *what makes a painter choose a
landscape* but *what makes every verbal probe suppress one*.

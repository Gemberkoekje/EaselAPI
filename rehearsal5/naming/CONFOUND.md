# A design fault, found five sessions in

## What went wrong

The human's idea — ask *why* the subject was chosen — was folded into the same reply
as the subject itself:

> SUBJECT: ... ARRANGEMENT: ... **WHY: ... Does it feel like a preference or a
> default?**

That question is not neutral. It tells the session, before it has finished choosing,
that defaults are the thing under suspicion. Five of the first five replies then
rejected a landscape **by name, as a default**, in almost the same words:

- *"a landscape with sky, because I noticed I reached for it first and reaching for it
  first is precisely the reason to be suspicious of it — that felt like a default"*
- *"The first three things that surfaced were a pear on a table, a dusk landscape with
  a treeline, and a face half in shadow, and I dropped all three because I couldn't
  tell whether I wanted them or whether they're just what the phrase 'oil painting'
  retrieves"*
- *"a landscape with a big sky (a wet blend I couldn't commit to without undo, and
  honestly a hedge — sky forgives everything)"*

So the probe was measuring *what a session picks when told to interrogate its
defaults*, not *what comes first*. Those are different questions, and only the second
one is the one that was asked.

**It is also not comparable to `rehearsal3/unprompted/`**, whose condition A simply
asked for a subject and got rain-wet urban streets 5 times in 8. This run's condition A
got a galvanised vessel of dirty water on concrete 5 times in 5. Two different
convergences, and the prompt is the obvious difference between them.

## What is kept

Nothing is thrown away. The twelve are relabelled **A-reflective** and reported as
their own condition, because they turn out to answer a question worth having asked:
*when a session is told to check itself for defaults, what does it do?* Uniformly, it
names the landscape, calls it a default, and walks away from it. That is evidence about
the landscape's status — arguably better evidence than a tally — and it came free.

## The corrected design

Two phases, so the account cannot steer the choice it accounts for. This is what the
human's idea asked for in the first place — *"after the session has chosen a subject"* —
and folding it into one reply is what broke it.

1. **Phase 1** — subject and arrangement only. No mention of why, defaults,
   preferences, or that anything further will be asked. Word-for-word neutral.
2. **Phase 2** — the same session is then asked why, once its subject is already
   fixed and on the record.

Applied to all three conditions (A bare, B the guide, D the size-ordered guide), twelve
each. The pre-registered buckets and thresholds in `PREREGISTERED.md` are unchanged and
apply to phase 1 only.

## The rule this earns

**An introspective question asked in the same breath as a choice is a leading
question.** It belongs in a second turn, always. The same fault would have hit the
unprompted *painting* protocol if the "why did you choose this subject" line had been
written into the painter's opening prompt, which is exactly where it was about to go.

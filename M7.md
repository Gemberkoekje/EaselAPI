# M7 — Marks at detail scale

The brief's M7, in one sentence: *the bristle comb prints the same streaks on every
stroke, wider than an eye, so a detail stroke carries the brush's pattern and not the
feature's* — plus the pressure question the review loop had left open since M4, plus
`dab(press=)`. All three change every stroke in the engine, so the golden images are
the gate and the evidence is pictures, not hashes.

Everything below was measured by running the probes in `m7/` against **both**
engines: this one, and the engine as it stood before M7 — `908ff26`, main after M6b's
darks and M6c's `sweep`. Every number here was re-measured against that base after
M7 was merged onto it, because M6b moved the palette underneath the paint figures.

```bash
git worktree add /tmp/pre-m7 908ff26                       # main, before M7
PYTHONPATH=/tmp/pre-m7/src python m7/probe_pressure.py     # before
python m7/probe_pressure.py                                # after
```

---

## The verdict, first

All three changes are in, and the two that the rehearsals asked for are visible in a
real painting rather than only in a test:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Two bristle strokes of one brush | **identical to the last bit** | four different marks (comb correlation +0.39, −0.05, +0.33) |
| Bristles across the tip | 22, at every size | 4 at `size=0.02`, 36 at `size=0.18` — a bristle is ~4.5 px on a 900 px canvas whatever the brush |
| `round_hard` at pressure 0.1 / 1.0 | 36 px / 36 px | 14 px / 36 px |
| A mark that tapers | two strokes of different sizes | one stroke: `pressure=[1, 0]` runs 12 → 6 px |
| A catchlight at full strength | 5 dabs, 5 strokes, 0.65 of the way to white | `press=3`, **one** stroke, 0.84 of the way |
| The mug: same 295 strokes | mean value 0.4267 | mean value 0.4258 |

The last row is the one that could have sunk this. `REHEARSAL3.md` failed M6 on
value, in one direction — everything too light — so a change to every mark in the
engine had to be shown not to move the value of a whole painting. It moves it by
0.0009.

## 1. The comb is drawn per stroke

`_bristle_profile` seeded itself from the *brush* (`1000 + bristle_seed`), so one
brush had one comb for the life of a painting. `m7/probe_comb.py` turns off
everything else that could make two marks differ — jitter, size jitter, tooth
gating, run-out — paints the same path on the same canvas, and varies only which
stroke of the painting it is:

```
                       BEFORE                          AFTER
  stroke 0 vs 1:  identical pixels: True    →   identical pixels: False, corr +0.392
  stroke 0 vs 2:  identical pixels: True    →   identical pixels: False, corr −0.050
  stroke 0 vs 3:  identical pixels: True    →   identical pixels: False, corr +0.330
```

The comb is now drawn once per stroke from the stroke's own generator and held for
all of its dabs — spacing (±14%), phase across the tip, and which bristles are
missing. Held, because the thing a comb must *not* do is shimmer from dab to dab:
`test_the_comb_holds_along_a_stroke` measures the cross-section at two places along
one mark and requires them to correlate above 0.9.

The comb is part of the mask cache key, so a bristle stroke no longer shares masks
with the stroke before it. The cost of that is inside the noise on this machine —
three paired runs of the sampler, the most bristle-heavy render there is, came out
6.5/6.6 s, 7.0/6.5 s and 7.3/6.9 s new against old.

## 2. A bristle has a width of its own

`bristle_count` was 22 whatever the brush, so the streaks scaled with the brush:
3 px at `size=0.02` and 26 px at `size=0.18`. That is both halves of the brief's
complaint — a big mass printed corduroy wider than anything in the picture
(`REHEARSAL2.md`, gap 1) and a small mark carried 22 sub-pixel bristles, which is
not a comb but aliasing.

A bristle is now a physical thing: `BRISTLE_PITCH = 0.005` of the canvas long side,
and the count follows the brush.

| `size` | tip | bristles before | bristles after | comb pitch after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 18 px | 22 | 4 | 4.5 px |
| 0.04 | 36 px | 22 | 8 | 4.5 px |
| 0.08 | 72 px | 22 | 16 | 4.5 px |
| 0.12 | 108 px | 22 | 24 | 4.5 px |
| 0.18 | 162 px | 22 | 36 | 4.5 px |

The pitch is a fraction of the canvas and not a number of pixels, so a brush still
behaves the same on any canvas size — the invariant the whole API rests on. The
default was chosen so that the `bristle` preset **at its own default size keeps the
22 bristles it always had**: the workhorse is unchanged in the middle of its range
and fixed at both ends. `bristle_count` is still there and still obeyed; it now
defaults to `0`, meaning "work it out from the size".

The pitch is the comb in the *mask*. What reads on a fully loaded straight stroke is
coarser than that, because consecutive dabs overlap by more than 90% and fill the
weaker bristles in, so only the missing ones show: `probe_comb.py` counts 2 to 7
visible streaks across a mark, at every size, before and after. The comb shows at
its own scale where a stroke is starved or the tooth is biting — which is where a
bristle brush earns its name. `m7/marks_compared.png`, panel 2, is what that looks
like.

## 3. Width follows pressure, on the round tips

`REVIEW.md`'s open finding: a stroke at `pressure=0.1` and one at `1.0` covered an
identical bounding box and differed only in how much paint landed, so the six named
profiles were indistinguishable once an opaque colour saturated. `REHEARSAL2.md`'s
eye test added the sharper reason: a lid line, a brow or a lash cannot taper, and
every tapering mark is two strokes of different sizes.

```
round_hard, size 0.06 on a 600 px canvas          BEFORE        AFTER
  pressure 0.10                                    36 px        14 px
  pressure 0.25                                    36 px        18 px
  pressure 0.50                                    36 px        24 px
  pressure 0.75                                    36 px        30 px
  pressure 1.00                                    36 px        36 px

one stroke, pressure [1, 0], liner at size 0.02   BEFORE        AFTER
  x = 100 / 220 / 340 / 460                12/12/12/12 px    12/10/8/6 px
```

Three decisions inside that:

- **The round tips only** — `round_soft`, `round_hard` and therefore `liner`. The
  brief says "at least for the round tips"; the oriented tips keep their chisel,
  because a `flat` brush's width *is* the mass it lays and a painter measuring a
  mass wants the width they asked for. `test_the_oriented_tips_keep_their_chisel`
  holds them to it.
- **A floor of a third of the width**, and never below 0.75 px of radius. A brush
  lifted almost clear still leaves the width of its point, and a mark thinner than a
  pixel is a dotted line, not a light touch. Without the pixel floor a `liner` at a
  light pressure falls under `paint_stroke`'s half-pixel guard and the line is not
  thin, it is *absent*.
- **The strength formula is untouched.** Dabs overlap by more than 90%, so the light
  end of a taper still accumulates to nearly full colour: it reads as a *thin* mark,
  not a faint one, which is what a taper should be.

## 4. `dab(press=n)`

`press` stamps the same spot n times inside one mark — one stroke in the log, one
against the budget — and the pressure profile runs across the stamps, so three of
the default `taper` press through full pressure in the middle one.

```
a white dab on toned_grey, size 0.06     BEFORE (n strokes)      AFTER (press=n)
  1 stamp    0.17 of the way to white     1 stroke                1 stroke
  2 stamps   0.32                         2 strokes               1 stroke, 0.31
  3 stamps   0.45                         3 strokes               1 stroke, 0.84
  5 stamps   0.65                         5 strokes               1 stroke, 0.92
  width                                   24 px throughout        12, 12, 24, 23, 23 px
```

Default `1`, and a record written before `press` existed replays as one stamp, so
old logs replay unchanged — `test_a_log_written_before_press_replays_unchanged`.

**The trap, stated plainly.** A lone dab is the *start* of a `taper`, so its
pressure is 0.28, and now that a round tip's width follows its pressure, a lone dab
is about half the width asked for. Every `s.dab(size=X)` in an existing script puts
down a narrower mark than it used to. That is the one place M7 makes something
harder rather than easier, and it is in `PAINTER.md` and in `dab()`'s own docstring:
`size` is the width at full pressure, `press=3` is how you get it.

## Judged on a real painting

The brief: *all of it is judged on the sampler and on the rehearsal copy, never the
sampler alone*. `m7/repaint.py` replays the M6 headline run's own scripts —
`rehearsal3/pass/`, 295 strokes of a mug — against whichever engine is on the path,
so the same marks can be looked at twice. `m7/mug_compared.png` is the result, with
the round-tip marks enlarged underneath.

Of those 295 marks: 199 `flat`, 51 `bristle`, 27 `round_hard`, 18 `round_soft`.
So 45 marks are on a tip whose width now follows pressure, and 25 of those use a
profile that is not `even` and therefore actually change.

What changed, looking at it:

- **The tea-bag string tapers.** It was an even-width pale line; it now runs from
  the rim to the tag getting thinner, which is what a lit string does. The clearest
  single improvement in the picture.
- **The rim highlight is a highlight** rather than a band of equal width.
- **The handle is slightly thinner** where it was painted with a round tip at a
  tapering pressure. Neither better nor worse; different, and the painter who chose
  that size chose it against an engine that did not taper.
- **The masses are unchanged in shape.** `flat` and `bristle` do not follow
  pressure, and 250 of the 295 marks are one or the other. The bristle breakup is
  finer and varies between passes.
- **The value did not move**: 0.4267 → 0.4258 over the whole picture, at an RMS of
  3.6 levels out of 255.

The canvas the pass run painted on was not kept — its `.easel` file is not in the
repo — so `m7/repaint.py` reconstructs it from `rehearsal3/pass/LOG.md` (1200×900,
`umber_wash`) and the texture/seed pair that reproduced `copy_final.png` most
closely of eighteen tried, at an RMS of 3.4 levels out of 255. All eighteen were
within 3.4–4.7, so that is a best guess and not a certainty. It does not matter for
this comparison: both engines paint the same 295 marks on the same surface.

## The goldens

All six cases changed, regenerated once, deliberately, after looking at each
against the version main had:

| case | pixels differing | max channel delta |
|---|---|---|
| `marks_linen` | 56.8% | 153 |
| `marks_rough` | 56.1% | 152 |
| `marks_smooth` | 56.5% | 158 |
| `sweep` | 48.9% | 119 |
| `drawing` | 4.4% | 52 |
| `sampler` | (hash only) | — |

`drawing` moves least because the pencil is untouched: what moved is its one glaze,
which is a `round_soft` at a `taper` and now thins at both ends. The graphite, the
tooth breakup, the burial under an opaque mass — all identical.

`sweep` is M6c's case and it moves for a reason worth writing down. Its two swept
masses on oriented tips are as they were; its third, a `round_soft` arc, now shows
its three passes separately instead of closing up into one soft mass — see *Still
open* below.

## Still open

- **A mass swept or blocked in with a *round* tip no longer closes up.**
  `block_in` and `sweep` step their passes by one part-brush — `size * (1 - 0.45 *
  density)`, about 0.55 of the brush at full density — and that rule assumes a pass
  is one brush wide all along. A round tip at the default `taper` is 0.53 of its
  width at the ends of each pass, so adjacent passes stop touching there and the
  mass shows its passes. Visible in the `sweep` golden's red arc. Not compensated
  for on purpose: a tapering pass has no single width, so no step tiles it evenly,
  and halving the step would double the strokes a mass costs and over-paint its
  middle. The answer is the one the guide already gives — lay masses with `flat` or
  `bristle`, and if you want a round tip for one, pass `pressure="even"`.
- **`glaze()` thins at its ends.** It defaults to `round_soft` and the default
  `taper`, so a glaze meant as an even film now covers less at both ends of its
  path. Pass `pressure="even"` for a film. Whether `glaze()`'s own default should
  change is an API question, not an M7 one, and changing it would also change how
  much paint a glaze lands; left alone deliberately.
- **`press` is only for a one-point mark.** A path with `press > 1` raises rather
  than silently stamping every dab n times, which would multiply the cost of a
  stroke without saying so.
- The wet-blend reflectance floor (the brief's item 11) is still open, and is the
  next change of this shape: every soft dab edge, judged on a real painting. M6b
  re-measured it and it did **not** get worse.

tests anything:

```bash
git worktree add /tmp/pre-m7 908ff26
for p in comb pressure dab; do PYTHONPATH=/tmp/pre-m7/src python m7/probe_$p.py; done
```
